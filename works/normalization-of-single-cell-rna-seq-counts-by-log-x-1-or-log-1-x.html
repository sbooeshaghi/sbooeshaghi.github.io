Normalization of single-cell RNA-seq counts by log (x+ 1) or log (1+ x)

Single-cell atlases are often used to reason about clinically important genes, but sparse counts make naive comparisons fragile. For low-abundance targets such as ACE2, filtering to nonzero cells and applying log1p transformations can create misleading impressions of expression. These examples clarify the statistical interpretation of positive-cell fractions and warn that common normalization choices can distort comparative estimates when their limitations are ignored.
