A transcriptomic and epigenomic cell atlas of the mouse primary motor cortex

Brain cell atlases are useful only when molecularly defined types are reproducible across assays and interpretable in anatomical context. Mouse primary motor cortex had fragmented transcriptomic and epigenomic evidence, including uncertainty about fine cell-type resolution and L4-like excitatory neurons. The atlas integrates more than 500,000 cells across RNA, DNA methylation, and chromatin-accessibility modalities, validates a reproducible taxonomy, links regulatory elements to cell types, and revises the view of MOp as strictly agranular.
