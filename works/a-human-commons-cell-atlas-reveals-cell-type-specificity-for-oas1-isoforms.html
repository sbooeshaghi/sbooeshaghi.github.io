A human commons cell atlas reveals cell type specificity for OAS1 isoforms

OAS1 isoforms matter because innate antiviral protection depends on which transcripts are present in the cell types a virus infects. Existing cell atlases largely report gene-level counts, making isoform-specific and dynamically reprocessable questions difficult. By building a 2.9-million-cell Human Commons Cell Atlas from raw FASTQs and reprocessing it at isoform resolution, the work identifies testis-specific OAS1-p44b expression in spermatogenesis cells and demonstrates a flexible atlas model for custom analyses.
